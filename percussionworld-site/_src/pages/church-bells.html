An article by Michel Mohnen

The Church Bells of Vijlen

My grandfather’s article about the bells in the church tower — published in the Vaalser Weekblad and rewritten by my father, Jean Mordant, one week before my mother passed away.

A hundred years ago these bells served as a means of communication — an early telephone or e-mail.

The three bells, tuned to D♯, F and G, told the villagers what was happening by ringing in a specific order.

The daily Mass, Lauds and Vespers were announced by the small bell (G) half an hour before they began, and again twenty minutes later. Halfway through Mass, three times three strokes on the small G bell told the people what was taking place in church. The small G bell also rang at daybreak, at noon and at 6 p.m. to mark the end of the working day.

On Sundays and holy days, and on both sad and joyful occasions, all the bells were rung.

When a poor person died, two bells rang three times with an interval; at the death of a wealthy person, three bells rang three times. I was pleased when this difference was abolished. At the death of a child, the small bell rang three times with an interval.

In case of fire, the village policeman climbed the tower and struck the small bell with a piece of metal for several minutes. This alerted the firemen and called on everyone available to come and help.

Ringing the bells was not easy: you had to be strong and learn the right technique.

History

The bells were taken by the Germans in 1943. In 1948 three new bells arrived in Vijlen. These bells, which still hang in the tower today, were cast by Van Bergen of Heiligerlee, in the province of Groningen.

A note from Michel Mordant

I had the opportunity to ring these bells myself, to announce weddings and funerals. I do not know whether my grandfather rang the bells as a professional.

Mr. Michel Mohnen, my granddad, lived in Vijlen from 1907 until 1989 and played the harmonica. He made my first drumsticks — I had a snare drum, but no sticks! — and bought my first guitar. He was the fundamental inspiration for my musical career, and his daughter, my mother, carried that inspiration on.

In honour of my grandfather and my mother.
— Michel Mordant
